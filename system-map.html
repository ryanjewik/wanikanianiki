Kanji Workshop · system design

Phone to Postgres and back

Every component, who talks to whom, and what one request actually turns into on its way through AWS.

01 · the whole system

Three zones and the lines between them

The phone holds a full local copy of what you are studying. AWS holds your backend, which wakes only when something calls it. WaniKani holds the truth about your SRS. Postgres sits underneath as the one thing that is always running.

The rule that shapes all of it: the phone never talks to WaniKani. If it did, the API token would have to live on the device.

02 · one request, end to end

What the data actually is at each hop

The routing happens twice, at two different layers, and keeping them apart is the thing that makes the whole design make sense.

AWS picks the function — based purely on which trigger fired. Every path that hits the Function URL goes to api-fn; AWS does no path matching at all. FastAPI picks the endpoint — from the path, using the decorators in routes.py. AWS never learns your routes exist.

03 · the three stores

Who owns which truth

There are three databases in this system and they are not peers. Only one of them holds anything you cannot get back.

why there are two SRS systems

WaniKani items keep WaniKani's stage. Everything else — photographed vocab, generated questions — runs your own SM-2 in srs_state. Keeping them separate means the two can never disagree about the same word: a WaniKani item's due date always comes from WaniKani, and yours always comes from you.

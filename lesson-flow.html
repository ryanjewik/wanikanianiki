Kanji Workshop · request flow

Anatomy of a Lesson

Every call the backend makes to WaniKani while you learn five new items — and the separate question of when any of it gets synced.

01 · the lesson

Three reads, then one write per item

A lesson is mostly reading. The only thing that changes anything on WaniKani's side is the very last step, and it happens once per item you finish.

Everything between loading the content and finishing runs with no network at all — that is the whole point of mirroring subjects onto the device.

02 · the budget

What a five-item lesson costs

WaniKani allows 60 requests per minute, counted against your token rather than your connection. Here is what one lesson batch actually spends, with the subject cache warm.

why not sync after each answer

A 40-item review session is already 40 writes. Add a sync pass after each one — each pass being a user call, an assignments page, and possibly a subjects page — and you are past 150 requests for a session that needed 40. The client would spend most of it asleep waiting on RateLimit-Reset.

Sync once when the session ends, not once per answer.

03 · the write, in detail

What comes back, and what doesn't

Both writes return the recomputed assignment, and the backend upserts it immediately. That is why no sync is needed to know the result of your own answer. But the two writes differ in what they leave unknown.

One more thing the backend does on a review that it does not do on a lesson: it appends a row to reviews_log. WaniKani's own POST /reviews returns id: 0 and keeps no history, so that table is the only durable record of what you answered and when. Your streak is derived from it, which is why the streak is empty without a database.

04 · sync

What one sync pass actually does

Sync is not for your own answers — those are already handled by the write responses above. It exists for everything the app did not cause: items unlocking on level-up, studying done on wanikani.com, resets, subscription changes. WaniKani has no webhooks, so it is poll-only.

05 · cadence

When sync runs

EventBridgesync_handler, 30 min Everything, with the app closed. Keeps the cache warm for the next open.
Each answer— Nothing. The write response already carries the authoritative state.

The ordering rule

The outbox drains before the pull, always. syncNow() in mobile/src/data/sync.ts already does this, and the comment there states the reason plainly: pull first and the incoming diff reflects a server that has not yet seen your answers, so it overwrites them with stale state. 

const writesReplayed = await replayPendingWrites();   // outbox first
const assignments = await api.fetchAssignments(lastSyncedAt);
await upsertAssignments(assignments);                  // then the diff

replayPendingWrites() stops at the first failure rather than skipping past it. Two reviews of the same item have to land in the order they were answered, so a delay is always better than a gap.

the split worth keeping

Your actions are synchronous and immediate. Finish a lesson, the write goes out now and comes back with the answer. Sync is for everything else — the state your app did not cause. Conflating the two is what produces both the redundant traffic and the stale-overwrite bug.
